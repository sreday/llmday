Scott Bastek

Teaching AI Systems to Fix Their Own Failures

PagerDuty
Prompt engineering has traditionally been manual trial-and-error. When AI agents degrade in production, teams make gut-feel changes and hope for the best. How do you know if a prompt change actually improves things, or just shifts failures elsewhere? We built an automated system that transforms production failures into quantifiable fixes. It starts by fetching examples of suboptimal sessions from our observability platform. The system analyzes these failures and clusters them into actionable patterns. For each pattern, it proposes specific prompt changes with before-and-after text. Then comes the critical step: validation. Only improvements that maintain or boost performance against our golden dataset make it into an automated pull request. Finally, the golden dataset itself is updated with representative samples of assessed failure. The result? We analyze all failures weekly and ship validated fixes—all without manual debugging. This approach transforms vague ""my AI isn't working"" complaints into concrete, measurable improvements backed by production data.... Read more

Philipp Pahl

Teaching Your Agent's Search Engine to Listen: Fine Tuning Retrieval on Proprietary Data

peachstone.ai
AI agents don't search like humans. They reason between every search call, and unlike humans, they're not lazy about spelling it out: what worked, what didn't, what to look for next. That chain of thought is rich retrieval signal that today's search engines throw away. Late interaction retrieval models can exploit that signal. Unlike single vector embeddings, which compress an entire document into one point in space, late interaction keeps a vector per token, capturing far more nuance with far smaller models. The tradeoff is more compute at search time, but efficient indexing techniques have largely solved that. And because these models are comparably small, they're fast and cheap to fine tune for your domain. That matters especially when you work with proprietary or specialized data where off the shelf models fall short: a generic retriever has never seen your internal documents, your terminology, your relevance criteria. Fine tuning bridges that gap. That's where frontier LLMs change the game. Training a retriever for your domain used to mean hiring annotators. Today, frontier models judge relevance at human evaluator quality and smaller, more affordable models can handle many of the same tasks at a fraction of the cost. I'll walk through a reproducible recipe: pool candidates with cheap retrievers, rerank them with LLM judges, extract training pairs. Then fine tune a late interaction model in minutes on a single rented GPU.... Read more
