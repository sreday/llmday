Aaron Zamora

Aaron Zamora

Cribl
Abstract

At the end of the month, your AI bill tells you what you spent, not whether you spent it well. Which queries were expensive? Which ones kept repeating? Are users even happy with the responses? Most teams have no idea. In this talk, I walk through a function that answers all three questions, even as AI logs flow through the telemetry pipeline. It extracts token counts, calculates per-query costs, flags cache candidates, and runs lightweight sentiment scoring on responses. Sentiment matters here - cheap queries that frustrate users aren't worth caching. Expensive queries with happy users might be worth keeping as-is. Then I show how this visibility reveals patterns. In one environment, a particular query might show up constantly: high frequency, low cost per query, and consistently positive sentiment - an obvious cache candidate. Meanwhile, other queries are expensive and generate frustrated responses. That's not a caching problem, that's a signal the model needs help. I'll walk through the function and code and show how it fits into any telemetry pipeline that supports in-flight transformation. Attendees leave with a working pattern they can adapt to their own stack. This session is for engineers who are suddenly responsible for AI infrastructure and need visibility before they can take action.

Bio

Aaron Zamora is a Principal Technical Marketing Engineer at Cribl and recovering Splunk engineer who likes shipping things more than talking about shipping things. He’s built demos, pipelines, and one fictional coffee company that became a company-wide Brand / Story. He’s also optimized Grafana monitoring to cut alert fatigue by 40%. When not wrangling telemetry, he’s using Grafana to figure out why some runs feel awful. Usually it’s sleep, heat, or beer. Sometimes all three.

Maciej Szymkowski

Maciej Szymkowski

Future Processing
Abstract

“Don't worry, most of you could be cured by a monkey with an aspirin.” Dr. Gregory House’s legendary cynicism used to target patient incompetence, but in 2026, it’s time to ask: should that monkey be replaced by an AI agent? In this session, we dive deep into the deployment of AI and classical ML across the biomedical and medical pipelines. We’ll skip the hype and look at the architectures. In Computer Vision we will check why RF-DETR is consistently outperforming YOLO in liver MRI and CT segmentation and detection of pathological changes. On the other hand, we will also consider Clinical NLP case where we leverage LLMs (Gemini, GPT, Bielik and Qwen) equipped with custom inference logic to parse patient interviews. We will also take a deeper look at drug discovery. We will observe how utilizing a multi-modal stack of VLMs and Vision models can accelerate the R&D lifecycle in that area. The presentation will be concluded by answering the ultimate question: Has the "Silicon Dr House" already arrived, or are we still just hallucinating the cure?

Bio

Maciej Szymkowski holds a PhD in Computer Science. Since April 2025, he has been working at Future Processing as a Senior Machine Learning Engineer. Previously, he was affiliated with Bialystok University of Technology (Faculty of Computer Science, 2018–2026), Warsaw University of Technology (Faculty of Electronics and Information Technology, 2021–2022), and AGH University of Science and Technology in Cracow (Faculty of Physics and Applied Computer Science, 2021–2022). He has also gained experience as a researcher on diverse projects and as a software developer and engineer in multiple companies, including SoftServe, Symmetra, Transition Technologies, and Hemolens Diagnostics. From 2022 to 2024, he worked at the Łukasiewicz Research Network, Poznan Institute of Technology, where he served as Head of the AI Development section. Maciej Szymkowski is the author or co author of more than 45 research papers published in JCR journals, book chapters, and international conference proceedings. His primary research interests lie in computer vision, particularly in medicine and transport, as well as Vision Language Models, Large Language Models, and machine learning algorithms. In his free time, he enjoys expanding his knowledge, taking long walks, reading books, and watching soccer, basketball, and hockey. He is a fan of Legia Warsaw, Real Madrid, the New York Knicks, and the Pittsburgh Penguins.

Lev Andelman

Lev Andelman

TeraSky
Abstract

Humans hallucinate memories, forget decisions, and confidently state wrong facts - and we built entire civilizations around it. Yet when AI does the same, we call it broken. This talk uses cognitive science, live audience experiments, and real-world engineering failures to expose the double standard killing your AI adoption. You’ll leave with a practical framework: validate the output, not the process - and stop demanding perfection from AI that you never demanded from yourself.

Bio

Lev Andelman is a Co-Founder and CTO at TeraSky Group with over 15 years of experience designing and managing large-scale production environments. He specializes in cloud, DevOps, and infrastructure across Unix/Linux and Windows systems, with a track record of leading engineering teams and building robust R&D, QA, and production platforms.
