Thalita Regina Terpilauskas de Sa

Riverstone Insights - Iowa
AI Isn’t Magic—But It Can Deliver Remarkable Results
Abstract

Artificial intelligence is often presented as a magical solution capable of solving any business problem. However, many organizations begin their AI initiatives without a clearly defined use case, reliable data, or a realistic understanding of what the technology can deliver. This talk explores two of the most common challenges in enterprise AI adoption: the gap between business expectations and technical reality, and the lack of structured, consistent, and contextualized data. It also discusses the role of technical professionals in transforming broad AI ambitions into measurable and achievable solutions. Finally, I will present a real-world predictive analytics project developed for the Brazilian consortium market. The case demonstrates how understanding the business problem, preparing the data, and selecting the right technical approach increased prediction accuracy from approximately 55% to 91.5%, turning an abstract AI initiative into a practical decision-support solution.

Bio

Thalita Regina Terpilauskas de Sa is a Business Intelligence, Data Analytics, and Automation professional with over 12 years of experience developing data-driven solutions for international organizations. Throughout her career, she has worked at leading global companies, including Louis Dreyfus Company, COFCO International, and Cargill. She is the founder and CEO of Riverstone Insights, a US-based data and AI consulting company that provides Business Intelligence, data analytics, automation, and AI solutions to clients such as HP, Italkraft, and DirectCon. Her expertise includes Power BI, SQL, Python, machine learning, predictive analytics, and business process automation. She recently completed the MIT IDSS Data Science and Machine Learning Program and focuses on transforming complex business challenges into practical, measurable technology solutions.

Marcelo B San Martin

Blizzard Entertainment
The Great AI Role Rewrite
Abstract

AI is no longer only automating tasks; it is beginning to rewrite the roles people play at work and in everyday life. As LLMs become collaborators, advisers, tutors, creators, and interfaces to knowledge, the value of many human roles is shifting from producing every output ourselves to directing AI systems, exercising judgment, validating results, and deciding what should not be delegated. This talk explores the changes already visible in software teams, management, education, creative work, and daily decision-making—and what they suggest about the broader societal transformation ahead. It also examines the risks that come with convenience, including weakened critical thinking, blurred accountability, and growing dependence on systems we do not fully understand. Rather than treating AI as simply another productivity tool, the talk presents it as the beginning of a wider redesign of work, institutions, and human identity. Attendees will leave with a practical framework for deciding what to delegate to AI, what should remain under human control, and which capabilities we need to strengthen as our roles continue to evolve.

Bio

Marcelo B. San Martin is a software engineer and QA automation leader with more than 13 years of experience, including work with Salesforce, PayPal, and Blizzard Entertainment. In recent years, he has focused on applied AI, LLM-powered systems, agentic workflows, and using AI to transform software engineering and quality practices. He also creates educational content exploring the AI revolution and its impact on work, creativity, critical thinking, and everyday life.

Leon Achata

TCS
RAG Is Not a Search Problem
Abstract

Modern RAG systems are often reduced to a simple pipeline: embed documents, perform vector search, retrieve the top-k chunks, and send them to an LLM. In practice, retrieval is rarely that simple. Exact identifiers, technical terminology, semantic paraphrases, lost document context, and multi-step questions can all expose weaknesses in a purely vector-based approach. This talk explores how modern retrieval systems move beyond basic semantic search through hybrid retrieval, combining sparse and dense methods, contextual retrieval, reranking, query decomposition, and adaptive or agentic retrieval. Rather than presenting a single “best” architecture, we will examine the trade-offs between relevance, recall, latency, cost, and complexity, and look at when each technique is actually useful. The central idea is simple: a RAG system does not need to find the most similar chunks; it needs to retrieve the right evidence for the question. We will build this idea step by step and show how retrieval quality, rather than simply choosing a more powerful LLM, can determine the quality of the final answer.

Bio

Leon Achata is a Generative AI Engineer focused on building production-grade LLM applications, RAG systems, and intelligent agents. He currently works on enterprise GenAI solutions at Apple via TCS, designing LLM-powered workflows, retrieval systems, and agent orchestration for large-scale international environments. Previously, he worked on multi-agent systems, RAG pipelines, and AI infrastructure across AWS and other cloud platforms. His background combines software engineering, AI/ML, and biomedical engineering, giving him a practical perspective on applying LLMs to complex real-world problems.

Josefina R. Casanova

IBM
From PDFs to Production RAG: Lessons from Building Document Pipelines with Docling
Abstract

Large Language Models have become remarkably capable, yet many AI projects still struggle to deliver reliable results. The problem is often not the model itself, but the quality of the information being provided to it. In this session, we'll take a practical look at document intelligence for production AI systems. Through real-world examples and a live demo using open source tools, we'll compare different approaches for transforming complex documents into structured knowledge ready for retrieval-augmented generation (RAG). Attendees will leave with practical techniques for improving retrieval quality, reducing hallucinations, and building more trustworthy AI systems. The session is tool-agnostic and highly practical, focused on lessons that can be immediately applied to real projects.

Bio

Josefina Casanova is a Data & AI Architect at IBM and a Computer Science Engineer passionate about using technology to solve real problems. She spends her days working at the intersection of AI, data and business, helping organizations build solutions that turn messy data into systems that work in production. A lifelong learner, she regularly speaks at tech events, hosts podcasts, and enjoys sharing lessons learned from building with emerging technologies.

Joaquin Bonifacino

Zyte
Long Running Agents: The Harness Is the Product
Abstract

Most agent demos run for thirty seconds. Production agents run for hours, and almost everything that breaks at that timescale has nothing to do with the model. Drawn from building an agentic data-QA engine running at 20B+ tokens per week in production. Why long-running agents fail: context degradation, silent tool failure, unrecoverable state, cost drift, and why demos never catch them. How harness design answers each one: compaction and memory, per-role model routing, sandboxed execution, checkpointing and recovery. Where to start: what to build first moving an agent from demo to production, and what to instrument on day one.

Bio

AI/ML Engineer at Zyte, technical lead for LLMNL, an AI-powered data-QA engine running agentic workloads at 20B+ tokens per week. Previously led Promtior's Augmented Behaviors Lab (applied R&D in agent memory, voice and multimodal). Teaches Applied AI at ORT University, Montevideo.

Ben Sterne

Match My Email
Beyond Vibe Coding: How AI is Changing Development Cycles
Abstract

Just a few years ago, all new software was designed and coded by humans. Today, AI tools like Claude Code, Codex, and GitHub Copilot are reshaping how software is built from the ground up. This talk explores how adopting an AI-native product development cycle transformed our team's workflow and boosted productivity. We will examine the specific engineering patterns, automated verification loops, and team workflows required to move reliably beyond informal 'vibe coding' into production-grade AI development.

Bio

Ben Sterne is the Product Lead for AI initiatives at Match My Email, exploring applied AI in the CRM space. He is an early adopter of AI technologies and built the first AI prototypes with top product and design executives at Salesforce and Slack to guide their AI strategies. He comes from a product design and data science background, building upon successful products as well as building new features and products from scratch. Day-to-day, he is building AI tools, drawing on 10 years of experience in the tech space working with companies like Slack, Salesforce, Sonos, and Google.

Martin Brian

Marvik
The Harness Auction: What Makes an AI Agent Actually Work?
Abstract

What makes an AI agent effective: a better model, the right tools, specialized skills, more context, subagents, or a verification loop? In this interactive talk, every audience member receives a limited budget and bids in a live auction for the components needed to assemble an agent harness. Participants then lock in their chosen combination and watch it enter a controlled benchmark gauntlet measuring correctness, regressions, safe execution, speed, and cost until only the winning harness remains.Through the results, we will explore which components materially improve an agent, which merely add cost and complexity, and why the system surrounding an LLM can matter as much as the model itself.

Bio

Martin Brian is a Senior AI Engineer specializing in the design, deployment, and operation of agentic systems in demanding production environments. At Marvik, he works as part of the AI team for a global payments platform, building agents that automate end-to-end software-development workflows across the organization.
