Vibes Don't Scale: Human-Driven Development in the Age of AI

Georgios Diamantopoulos
Zero to MVP

Low-code promised anyone could build software. It was true, until you hit a real wall: the blocks ran out, you needed custom code, you called an engineer.

Vibe coding has no visible wall. A founder used AI tools for six weeks and produced 300,000 lines of code; working UI, real users, flawless surface. Underneath: security holes, queries returning entire tables to count, and a codebase three engineers couldn't untangle.

He didn't know he'd hit the wall; it looked like a working product! The constraints appeared to disappear when they just shifted to security, maintainability, performance and UX. Handing 300,000 lines to an engineering team and asking them to make it work — from experience — is borderline traumatic. You just devalued our entire careers by handing us this. Decades of perfecting a craft, dismissed by a chatbox.

Here's where it gets interesting. The map is not the territory — you have to build things to understand what you're building, and AI makes that exploration faster than ever. This is a skill issue — just not the one people think; the skill is recognising when the prototype became the product, and making sure it can bear the weight. That knowledge just went from nice-to-have to last line of defense.

Constraining AI before it writes, not after — that's what I call Human-Driven Development. Through live AI-assisted demos, I'll show how the tools engineers spent years mastering are the difference between software that ships and software that survives.

Georgios Diamantopoulos, PhD, is a technology founder and automation strategist with 15+ years of experience building scalable software systems and more than 35 MVPs for startups and growth-stage companies. As Founder & CEO of Zero to MVP, he helps businesses accelerate product development, streamline operations, and sustain release momentum through zero-friction automation. His background spans software engineering, technical consulting, fintech, and systems architecture, with prior leadership roles across startups and trading technology firms. He also holds a PhD from the University of Birmingham, where he developed advanced eye-tracking systems.
