Erik Schwartz

Erik Schwartz

TheAiExpert.ai

This year I argued that context engineering is the real power tool behind great prompts. Next year I want to show what happens when you take that seriously and push it all the way to shipping real software in a single day.

In this session we will move from theory to practice. I will unpack the context patterns that actually hold up once you leave the playground and build for real users. Multi layer memory, data boundaries, llms.txt style controls, retrieval patterns that survive messy SharePoint and inboxes, and the review loops you need so legal, compliance and IT can sleep at night.

Then I will connect it to something very practical. My “Ship Your First App In A Day” workshops, where non technical leaders go from “what is a terminal” to a deployed, context aware AI app by 5pm. I will break down the scaffolding that makes that possible. How we design prompts around specific jobs to be done, how we shape context so models stay on track, and how we hand these experiments over to professional engineering teams without losing the magic.

You will leave with a concrete blueprint to take your own prompt engineering practice from clever instructions on a screen to working AI apps that your business can actually use.

Erik Schwartz is a technology leader, entrepreneur, and founder of theAiExpert.ai, with over 25 years at the intersection of search, knowledge discovery, and artificial intelligence. He has held senior roles at Comcast, Elsevier, and Microsoft, where he pioneered large-scale platforms and introduced generative AI into mission-critical products used by millions. At Elsevier, Erik spearheaded ScopusAI, a breakthrough generative AI solution that transformed how researchers discover and evaluate knowledge — a project that exemplifies his talent for turning cutting-edge innovation into measurable business value. Today, through theAiExpert.ai, Erik acts as a Fractional Chief AI Officer (CAIO), helping organisations move from AI-curious to AI-powered. He designs agentic workflows, compliance accelerators, and adaptive AI systems that cut costs, reclaim time, and unlock growth. A regular speaker, writer, and host of the London AI Dinner, Erik is recognised for his ability to demystify AI for executives, inspire bold thinking, and deliver strategies that bridge experimentation with impact.

Dr David Crepaz-Keay

Dr David Crepaz-Keay

The Mental Health Foundation

As generative AI is increasingly applied to mental health, the risks of harm, misinformation, and inappropriate support grow alongside its potential benefits. This presentation describes the Mental Health Foundation’s approach to building a mental health support bot that is safe, ethical, and effective—placing mental health expertise at the centre of prompt engineering.

The work builds on the speaker’s experience developing the original Public Health England Mind Plan tool, now hosted by NHS England, and applies established public mental health principles to large language model design. The talk explores how prompt engineering can be used to define clear role boundaries, prevent diagnostic or therapeutic overreach, respond appropriately to distress, and ensure consistent signposting to human support.

Rather than optimising for fluency alone, the Mental Health Foundation’s approach uses prompt constraints, tone guidance, and escalation logic to prioritise safety, trust, and real-world use in sensitive contexts. The session also reflects on collaboration between mental health specialists and technical teams, demonstrating how domain-led prompt design reduces risk while enabling genuinely supportive interactions.

This presentation offers a practical case study in building mental health bots that are helpful without being unsafe—and shows why mental health applications require more than generic AI capability.

Dr David Crepaz-Keay, FRSPH, is Head of Research and Applied Learning at the Mental Health Foundation, a 75 year old public mental health NGO. He leads the London based research team and is responsible AI, LLMs and knowledge management systems for the organisation.

Dr Crepaz-Keay is a former co-chair and now member of the ethics, policy and position committee of the International Society for Psychiatric Genetics and a fellow of the Royal Society for Public Health. He is an editor of the Journal of Mental Health Training, Education and Practice; the Oxford Handbook of Philosophy and Public Mental Health and the Handbook of Phenomenology, Values and Clinical Decision-Making in Personalised Mental Health Care. He has been a technical advisor for the World Health Organisation, senior mental health advisor to Public Health England, written a mental health module for the Open University, written, spoken and campaigned for improvements in mental health services.

Michele Sciabarra

Michele Sciabarra

Nuvolaris

In this talk we show how we built Trustable, a fully private AI platform that delivers an experience similar to Lovable, while keeping everything under the company’s control.

No opaque clouds. No closed models. No external AI services quietly siphoning your data.

Starting from Apache OpenServerless and OpenCode, we walk through how to design and implement a system that:

A practical story about building AI that feels lovable to users, but is genuinely trustable for organizations.

Maciej Szymkowski

Maciej Szymkowski

Future Processing

Battle of the bots: VLMs (Qwen 3, InternVL-3.5) vs. YOLOv11 + PaddleOCR. Using 2,500 toll booth images of vehicles, the task covers car vs. truck classification and license plate recognition. Evaluation includes accuracy, precision, and Levenshtein distance to determine which pipeline truly owns the road. Who wins?

Max Borodin

Max Borodin

Simple App

AI chatbots are everywhere, but most get ignored after a week. This talk shares the production architecture and practical patterns behind a successful AI Coach in a health & fitness app with 21M downloads — winner of MedTech's Best Virtual Health Coach 2025. Max Borodin walks through the end-to-end design: defining the core "jobs" a coach must do for users, structuring scenarios with memory and context engineering (beyond simple prompts), and embedding coaching into product flows with UX rituals that feel natural rather than bolted-on. He contrasts agentic architectures (multi-step reasoning, tool calling) vs completion-centric (single-turn responses) and their trade-offs for latency, cost, and reliability. The session dives into measurement and iteration: quantitative engagement metrics (daily active conversations, session length), qualitative evals (ratings, transcript review), pre-testing frameworks, and rapid scenario management to continuously improve personality, tone, and outcomes. Attendees will leave with a checklist for building their own coach: specific patterns, pitfalls to avoid, and optimisation techniques that make AI sustainable at scale. Perfect for developers and PMs turning LLMs into product features that stick.

Max Borodin is a Senior Product Manager at Simple App, where he leads AI coaching and gamification initiatives. He has a background in economics from Lomonosov Moscow State University and experience spanning product management and consulting, including an early role at McKinsey & Company.
